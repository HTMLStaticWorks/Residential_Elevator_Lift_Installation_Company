Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common structural, electrical, and maintenance queries about home lifts. *Contact custom service lines for specific code approvals.*

Space requirements depend strictly on the selected model. Compact pneumatic circular lifts require a circular footprint of about 950 mm. Traditional glass villa traction models typically require a masonry or glass shaft structure of at least 1200 x 1200 mm inner clear space.
Yes. Retrofitting is common. Compact pneumatic lifts require zero concrete pits or overhead room adjustments, allowing installation inside staircase voids. Other models can be installed by cutting floor openings or mounting an exterior weatherproof glass shaft on the building facade.
Most residential elevators serve between 2 to 5 stops (up to 15 meters travel height). High-performance gearless traction models can serve up to 6 stops if structural constraints allow.
Capacity ranges from 250 kg (2-3 persons) on compact circular pneumatic models, to 400-450 kg (4-6 persons) on premium hydraulic and traction villa lifts. Heavy-duty structures can support up to 630 kg (8 persons).
Most modern residential lifts operate on a standard single-phase 220V power supply. Larger traction or high-capacity hydraulic pump units may require a three-phase 380V supply.
Physical installation of pneumatic lifts takes approximately 3 to 4 days on-site. Standard glass shaft villa elevators require 2 to 3 weeks for mechanical assembly, panel adjustment, electrical routing, and safety certifications testing.
Pneumatic vacuum models require almost no structural modification, only floor cutouts. Traction and hydraulic elevators require building a vertical shaft (masonry or steel frame) and a shallow concrete pit (50mm to 200mm) at the bottom level.
To ensure safety margins, periodic inspections are recommended. Typically, 2 to 4 inspections per year are optimal to check wire rope tensions, lubricate guide tracks, and calibrate electronic safety sensors.
Our elevators incorporate emergency battery lowering backups. In a power outage, the cabin automatically descends to the next lower landing level, aligns doors, and unlocks them to allow passenger exit.
